## Page 03 — Reach: Tool-Free Under-Sink Caddy

**Status:** Founding candidate / concept / not for sale.  
**Job:** Bring frequently used bottles forward without drilling around plumbing.  
**Intended user:** A dry cabinet with measured clearance and a small group of frequently used supplies.

### Do not buy for

- An active leak, mold, odor, or pest issue.
- Unsafe chemical combinations.
- A cabinet that is simply holding too much.
- Clearance that has not been measured.

### Why this form

- Freestanding and removable.
- Narrow enough to work around plumbing.
- Designed to carry one useful group.
- Washable if a bottle leaks.

### Alternatives

**Spend less:** use a plain handled bin when containment and lifting solve the problem.  
**Spend more:** use an installed pull-out only when greater capacity justifies commitment to the cabinet.

### Unknowns and gates

Final dimensions, load limit, handle and base durability, materials, cleaning behavior, landed cost, and return risk remain unknown. A real sample must fit around plumbing, remain stable when moved, contain a small leak, and survive 500 pull/lift/reset cycles.

## Page 04 — Retrieve: Pull-Out Pantry Caddy

**Status:** Founding candidate / concept / not for sale.  
**Job:** Move one hidden pantry category from a deep shelf to the counter.  
**Intended user:** A measured shelf with one recurring category that is difficult to see or reach.

### Do not buy for

- Active pests or uncleaned spills.
- Excess inventory disguised as a storage problem.
- A shallow or weak shelf.
- Loose heavy items outside the declared load.

### Why this form

- Pulls one category out as a unit.
- Dividers prevent a new pile.
- High walls contain small packages.
- No cabinet hardware required.

### Alternatives

**Spend less:** use a clear bin when visibility and occasional lifting are enough.  
**Spend more:** use an installed drawer when higher capacity justifies hardware, fit work, and permanence.

### Unknowns and gates

Wheel durability, loaded pull force, shelf compatibility, usable width, and willingness to pay over a basic bin remain unknown. A real sample must roll straight, avoid scratching, hold its dividers, and survive 1,000 loaded pull-and-return cycles.

## Page 05 — Reset: Expandable Drawer Organizer

**Status:** Founding candidate / concept / not for sale.  
**Job:** Give everyday utensils stable zones that adapt to the existing drawer.  
**Intended user:** A measured drawer with useful utensils that fit its depth and top clearance.

### Do not buy for

- An overfilled drawer that needs a clean-out.
- A shallow or obstructed drawer.
- Utensils taller than safe clearance.
- A drawer where a fixed tray already fits.

### Why this form

- Adjusts to available width.
- Moves with the customer rather than altering the drawer.
- Creates visible zones.
- Removes for cleaning.

### Alternatives

**Spend less:** use a fixed tray when it already fits.  
**Spend more:** use a custom insert only when maximum space use earns lower flexibility and higher commitment.

### Unknowns and gates

Expansion-lock durability, slip resistance, material odor, cleaning, and real utensil fit remain unknown. A real sample must hold its width through 1,000 cycles, remain in place, preserve top clearance, and avoid sharp edges, odor, or trapped-water channels.

## Page 06 — Our recommendation standard

**Headline:** Confidence comes from useful limits.

Five gates:

1. Name the real problem.
2. Show who should not buy.
3. Make fit knowable.
4. Compare honest alternatives.
5. Verify before making a claim.

**Working philosophy:** A product earns a place only when it solves the right problem, fits the real space, and is better than buying nothing.

## Page 07 — About

**Headline:** We are not here to reinvent the home.

Little Built is described as the working brand for Company 001, ACE's first case study. The prototype begins with reversible organization because customers can improve awkward spaces without construction or permanent commitment.

- Useful over impressive.
- Reversible over disruptive.
- Truth over urgency.

## Page 09 — Shipping and returns

**Status:** Draft principles only—not current commercial terms.

The shipping side requires full cost before payment, honest delivery estimates, tracking when available, no unsupported same-day claims, and a plain-language damage process.

The return side prioritizes complete fit guidance, rejects universal-fit claims, requires a visible return window, and separates defect, damage, and preference returns.

Final terms require a selected product, fulfillment path, real shipping cost, and legal review appropriate to the launch market.
