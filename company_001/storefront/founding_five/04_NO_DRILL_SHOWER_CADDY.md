# Founding Product 04 — No-Drill Shower Caddy

**Status:** reserve; do not source until launch trio fails or reserve risk is materially reduced

## What problem does it solve?

Bathrooms without built-in shelves force bottles onto floors and ledges. A removable caddy adds vertical storage without drilling.

## Who is it for?

Someone with a manufacturer-approved clean, smooth surface who will follow preparation, cure-time, load, and removal instructions.

## Who should not buy it?

Anyone with an unapproved, porous, damaged, textured, wet, freshly painted, or uncertain surface—or anyone unwilling to accept residual surface risk.

## Why this one?

The desired specification uses documented wet-environment mounting, replaceable mounting components, corrosion-resistant material, drainage, and a conservative load limit. 3M publishes compatible surfaces and a 6.5-pound claim for one Command model ([3M](https://www.command.com/3M/en_US/p/d/b5005604030/)).

## Why not a cheaper alternative?

Unverified adhesive and vague “rustproof” claims can turn a cheap product into broken bottles, damaged surfaces, and returns. A cheaper caddy is better only when its material and mounting evidence are equally strong.

## Why not a more expensive alternative?

A properly installed fixed shelf is better for an owner seeking permanent high-load storage. This product is for reversibility, not maximum capacity.

## What evidence supports the recommendation?

Home Depot carries many adhesive, suction, hanging, and fixed caddies across a broad price band, while 3M publishes surface and installation limits. That establishes a real comparison category, not a reason to sell a generic version.

## What remains unanswered?

Supplier documentation, corrosion testing, adhesive aging, removal damage, replacement-strip availability, claim liability, and return rate.

## Sample checks

- reject unless the complete mounting system has traceable documentation;
- 30-day wet/humidity load test at the proposed conservative limit;
- accelerated corrosion and coating inspection;
- removal test on every claimed surface type;
- drainage, cleaning, sharp-edge, hook, and bottle-stability checks;
- packaging must make disqualifying surfaces impossible to miss.
