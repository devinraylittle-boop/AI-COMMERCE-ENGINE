# ACE Interview Protocol v0.1 — Calibration Draft

**Status:** training only  
**Permitted use:** two Friction Removal pilot interviews  
**Prohibited use:** mechanism gates, market prevalence, product selection, or commercial claims

## Calibration objective

Determine whether the interview method produces traceable accounts of recent behavior without leading participants toward ACE's Friction Removal hypothesis.

The pilots test the method, not the mechanism.

## Participant screen

Recruit a person who, during the last six months, tried to organize or improve a household storage area and either bought something, built a workaround, abandoned the effort, or returned a solution.

Do not disclose the mechanism. Say:

> I'm testing how I conduct customer-research interviews. I want to understand something you actually did, not sell you anything. With your permission, I'll take notes without including unnecessary identifying information. You may skip anything or stop at any time.

Record verbal consent. Do not photograph a home, collect measurements, or record audio during the calibration pilots.

## Interview sequence

### Context

1. Think of the most recent household area you tried to organize. What was happening?
2. When was this?
3. What made you act at that particular moment?

### Behavior

4. Walk me through what you did, step by step.
5. What did you try first?
6. Did you buy, reuse, build, return, or abandon anything?
7. How did you choose that approach?

### Evidence and consequences

8. What information did you use before deciding?
9. What was difficult to know in advance?
10. What happened after you tried it?
11. What did the attempt cost in time, money, wasted space, or frustration?
12. Is the system still being used the same way today?

### Disconfirmation

13. Was any part of the problem caused by having too many things rather than inadequate storage?
14. What would have made buying another organization product a bad decision?
15. If you did nothing, what would have happened?

### Close

16. What important question did I fail to ask?
17. May I contact you once to confirm that I understood your account correctly?

Do not ask whether the participant would buy a proposed product. Do not introduce containers, fit guidance, modular systems, or ACE's theory unless the participant raised them independently.

## Note labels

Label every material note as one of:

- `VERBATIM` — the participant's words
- `BEHAVIOR` — an action or observable outcome
- `OPINION` — a belief, preference, or hypothetical statement
- `INFERENCE` — the interviewer's interpretation
- `CONTRADICTION` — evidence against the expected mechanism
- `UNKNOWN` — missing or ambiguous information

Never turn an inference into a quote or behavior.

## Stop conditions

Stop or redirect if:

- consent is withdrawn;
- the conversation enters unnecessary health, financial, family-conflict, or other sensitive detail;
- the participant expects professional organizing advice;
- the interviewer begins pitching, defending, or explaining a solution;
- notes can no longer distinguish participant language from interviewer interpretation.

## Required debrief

Complete `research/templates/INTERVIEW_DEBRIEF.md` immediately after each pilot. Compare both debriefs before creating v1.

Promote this protocol to v1 only when:

- the opening is understood;
- the interview fits within 25 minutes;
- participants can reconstruct a real event;
- leading questions and unclear prompts are identified and corrected;
- notes consistently separate behavior, language, opinion, inference, and contradiction;
- consent and privacy handling are workable.
