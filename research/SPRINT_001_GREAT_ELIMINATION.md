# Sprint 001 — The Great Elimination

**Operation Lemonade**  
**Decision date:** 2026-07-14  
**Decision:** Advance three markets to finalist evidence collection; do not select products yet.

## Executive decision

Advance these three markets:

1. **Dog Training & Enrichment** — strongest combination of durable demand, emotional commitment, replenishment, dense customer language, and brand expansion. Win on **safe, candid guidance**, not novelty.
2. **Home Espresso / Coffee** — frequent ritual, enthusiast progression, consumables, education, and a clear ladder from beginner to advanced. Win on **workflow clarity and honest value**, not gadget volume.
3. **Home Organization & Storage** — broad observed pain, simple nonregulated goods, low-cost testing, and the highest automation potential. Win on **fit certainty and simpler systems**, not more containers.

This is a portfolio decision, not a claim that these are the three largest markets. The finalists expose ACE to three different buying systems—care, ritual, and household friction—while retaining a credible path to a lean first offer.

Do **not** advance Kitchen Organization separately in the next sprint. It scores well, but overlaps heavily with Home Organization. Include the kitchen as one research context inside Home Organization so the first finalist portfolio does not double-count the same demand system.

## Decision standard

ACE exists to help us make better decisions than we could have made yesterday.

This sprint therefore optimizes for decision quality, not research volume. It uses public evidence to eliminate weak markets, exposes unknowns, and proposes the cheapest experiment that could change each conclusion. It does not authorize inventory, advertising, supplier outreach, or a storefront.

### Evidence rules

- **Observed:** supported by a cited first-party, government, trade-association, or method-disclosed source.
- **Inferred:** a reasoned conclusion from observed facts; not a measured market fact.
- **Hypothesis:** plausible but unverified; must be tested before it can drive spending.
- **Unknown:** public evidence was insufficient. Unknowns are not converted into numbers.
- Broad market figures are **context proxies**, not estimates of the narrower accessory opportunity.
- “Review density” and “supplier availability” are preliminary qualitative assessments. They require authorized marketplace samples and supplier verification in the next sprint.
- No category-wide average order value (AOV) was found in sufficiently trustworthy public sources. “Basket structure” is used instead of invented AOV.

## Market briefs

### 1. Dog Training & Enrichment

**Observed context.** The American Pet Products Association reports $158 billion in U.S. pet spending in 2025, projected to reach $165 billion in 2026. It estimates 71 million U.S. dog-owning households in 2025, while also reporting value pressure: 22% of pet owners said they were spending less. This supports a large, resilient broad market but also argues against undifferentiated premium pricing. ([APPA 2026 State of the Industry](https://americanpetproducts.org/news/u.s.-pet-industry-reaches-158-billion-in-2025-poised-for-continued-growth-in-2026))

- **Growth trend:** Positive but mature; value sensitivity is real.
- **Passion:** Very high; care and behavior are emotionally consequential.
- **Repeat purchase:** High through treats, replacement toys, rotating enrichment, and life-stage needs.
- **Basket structure:** Low-cost repeat items can ladder into higher-value training systems; category AOV is unknown.
- **Shipping complexity:** Low to medium for compact, nonfood accessories.
- **Return risk:** Medium; sizing, durability, dog preference, and unmet behavior expectations.
- **Review density:** Expected very high; requires authorized sample verification.
- **Supplier availability:** Expected high; quality and safety verification matter more than count.
- **Content opportunity:** Exceptional—training technique, safety, fit, age, breed tendencies, and enrichment rotation.
- **Automation suitability:** Medium-high if products avoid food, medical claims, complex sizing, and unsupervised-use promises.
- **Private-label potential:** High after safety and durability testing.
- **Brand-building potential:** High around trusted selection and explicit “who should not use this” guidance.
- **Recurring complaints:** Durability mismatch, choking or ingestion hazards, wrong sizing, hard-to-clean products, puzzles that are too easy or frustrating. AKC guidance explicitly emphasizes durability, correct sizing, supervision, and ingestion risk. ([AKC toy safety guide](https://www.akc.org/canine-partners/a-guide-to-toy-shopping-for-your-pup/))
- **Recurring wishes:** Honest difficulty levels, stronger safety guidance, washable construction, replacement parts, and products matched to chewing style and training stage. These are hypotheses to quantify.
- **Estimated validation cost:** **$0–$25** for structured review analysis and five owner interviews; no traffic spend yet.
- **Reasons not to enter:** Liability and customer-trust damage from unsafe products; commoditization; individual dog behavior makes universal claims unreliable; food and supplements add regulatory complexity.
- **Cheapest meaningful experiment:** Analyze an authorized sample of at least 100 reviews across three enrichment subcontexts, code durability/safety/cleaning/difficulty complaints, then interview five owners to test whether the taxonomy matches lived problems.

**If we enter this market:** win on **safe, candid matching and progressive enrichment**, not price or toy novelty.

### 2. Home Espresso / Coffee

**Observed context.** The National Coffee Association reported that 66% of American adults drank coffee in the past day in Spring 2025; an older Fall 2025 public release reported 82% of coffee drinkers prepared coffee at home and specialty consumption had risen materially since 2021. The NCA warns that public snapshots are not substitutes for its paid full study, so these figures establish participation—not accessory revenue. ([NCA National Coffee Data Trends](https://www.ncausa.org/Research-Trends/National-Coffee-Data-Trends), [NCA Fall 2025 snapshot](https://www.ncausa.org/Newsroom/Grounds-for-celebration-Americans-remain-committed-to-coffee))

- **Growth trend:** Stable mass habit with a growing specialty segment; equipment fashion may cycle faster than coffee demand.
- **Passion:** Very high among home-barista segments.
- **Repeat purchase:** Very high in beans, filters, cleaning, water treatment, and maintenance; lower for durable gear.
- **Basket structure:** Broad ladder from low-cost workflow accessories to expensive machines; niche AOV unknown.
- **Shipping complexity:** Low for compact accessories; high for machines and fragile glassware.
- **Return risk:** Medium; compatibility, expectations, measurement precision, and technique are frequent failure points.
- **Review density:** Expected very high; verify by authorized product-class samples.
- **Supplier availability:** High, but generic accessories face price competition and design-copying.
- **Content opportunity:** Exceptional—dial-in, water, cleaning, workflow, diagnosis, and beginner progression.
- **Automation suitability:** Medium-high for universal, compact, non-electrical accessories; lower for machine-specific parts.
- **Private-label potential:** Medium-high for simple workflow and maintenance goods; avoid pressure-bearing or electrical parts first.
- **Brand-building potential:** High if recommendations are machine-aware and resist unnecessary upgrades.
- **Recurring complaints:** Mess, inconsistent extraction, confusing compatibility, cleaning burden, wasted beans, and gear that improves aesthetics rather than coffee. These are strong hypotheses requiring review quantification.
- **Recurring wishes:** Clear beginner paths, compatible bundles, measurable improvement, less counter mess, and honest “good enough” guidance.
- **Estimated validation cost:** **$0–$30** for review mining, five workflow interviews, and one problem-ranking survey.
- **Reasons not to enter:** Sophisticated customers punish weak expertise; saturated accessory listings; trademarks and compatibility language; expensive equipment support; faddish gadgets.
- **Cheapest meaningful experiment:** Compare 100 authorized reviews from beginner workflow accessories with 100 from maintenance accessories; test whether “mess/cleaning/compatibility” produces a more consistent pain cluster than “better extraction.”

**If we enter this market:** win on **education, compatibility, and fewer wasted upgrades**, not gadget count.

### 3. Houseplants & Indoor Gardening

**Observed context.** USDA NASS reports $6.71 billion in 2024 U.S. floriculture sales, up less than 1% from 2023; indoor/patio foliage plants accounted for $928.4 million among operations with at least $100,000 in sales. That is a useful industry proxy, not an accessories estimate. ([USDA 2024 Floriculture Highlights](https://www.nass.usda.gov/Publications/Highlights/2026/2024-floriculture-highlights.pdf))

- **Growth trend:** Broad wholesale market was essentially flat in 2024; accessory niches may differ.
- **Passion:** High, visually social, and collection-oriented.
- **Repeat purchase:** Medium-high through soil amendments, pest monitoring, supports, propagation, and repotting.
- **Basket structure:** Mostly low- to medium-ticket accessories with bundling potential; AOV unknown.
- **Shipping complexity:** Low for tools/supports; medium-high for soil, liquids, ceramics, lights, or live plants.
- **Return risk:** Low-medium for universal tools; higher for fragile décor and electronics.
- **Review density:** Expected high but fragmented by plant/problem.
- **Supplier availability:** High for generic accessories; quality differentiation is uncertain.
- **Content opportunity:** Exceptional—diagnosis, light, watering, pests, propagation, and seasonal care.
- **Automation suitability:** High if live plants, pesticides, heavy media, and fragile pots are excluded initially.
- **Private-label potential:** Medium-high for compact care systems.
- **Brand-building potential:** High around “prevent the common mistake” education.
- **Recurring complaints:** Overwatering, poor drainage, fungus gnats, light mismatch, pest spread, and confusing care advice. University of Minnesota Extension links overwatering and poor drainage to root rot and fungus gnats and emphasizes early detection. ([UMN indoor plant pest guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/yard-and-garden-insects/insects-indoor-plants))
- **Recurring wishes:** Simple diagnostics, watering confidence, clean repotting, earlier pest detection, and less contradictory guidance.
- **Estimated validation cost:** **$0–$20** for a care-problem survey plus authorized review classification.
- **Reasons not to enter:** Flat broad-market growth, misinformation and pesticide-claim risk, fragile/heavy goods, seasonal demand, and cheap commodity competition.
- **Cheapest meaningful experiment:** Ask 25 plant owners to rank their last three preventable failures, then test whether review themes cluster by care stage rather than plant species.

**If we enter this market:** win on **prevention and calm diagnosis**, not plant-parent aesthetics.

### 4. Van / RV Organization

**Observed context.** RV Industry Association data shows approximately 8.1 million RV-owning U.S. households, median owner age 49, and median use of 30 days per year. Industry shipments reached 342,220 units in 2025, 2.5% above 2024, with 2026 forecast near 349,300. This is a meaningful installed base, but post-pandemic normalization—not explosive unit growth. ([RVIA Market Pulse](https://www.rvia.org/market-pulse-dashboard), [RVIA 2025 shipments](https://www.rvia.org/reports-trends/rv-shipment-reports/2025-12/rv-shipments-end-2025-342220-units-modest-25-growth-over-2024))

- **Growth trend:** Modest current growth with a large installed base.
- **Passion:** High among frequent users; use is episodic for many owners.
- **Repeat purchase:** Medium; upgrades follow trips, vehicle changes, and discovered space problems.
- **Basket structure:** Medium ticket with bundle potential; AOV unknown.
- **Shipping complexity:** Medium; compact organizers are easy, rigid systems and hardware are not.
- **Return risk:** Medium-high due to dimensions, layout, mounting, vibration, and vehicle-specific fit.
- **Review density:** Expected medium-high but fragmented by vehicle class and floorplan.
- **Supplier availability:** Medium-high; generic home organizers may not survive movement.
- **Content opportunity:** High—measurement, fit, trip workflow, installation, and before/after demonstrations.
- **Automation suitability:** Medium; fit capture and installation support are constraints.
- **Private-label potential:** Medium if modular and non-structural.
- **Brand-building potential:** High around space certainty and road-tested recommendations.
- **Recurring complaints:** Wasted vertical space, items shifting in transit, inaccessible storage, incompatible dimensions, weak adhesives, and difficult installation. These remain hypotheses pending review data.
- **Recurring wishes:** Exact measurements, floorplan-aware guidance, tool-free mounting, modularity, and proof under vibration.
- **Estimated validation cost:** **$10–$35** for interviews, dimensional problem capture, and one no-inventory concept test.
- **Reasons not to enter:** Fragmented fit matrix, seasonal use, installation liability, higher support burden, and bulky returns.
- **Cheapest meaningful experiment:** Interview five owners from different RV classes and map every organization failure to location, dimensions, travel state, and installation tolerance; reject any problem without cross-layout recurrence.

**If we enter this market:** win on **measured fit and movement-proof systems**, not generic storage labeled “RV.”

### 5. Consumer 3D Printing

**Observed context.** Market-intelligence firm CONTEXT reported that entry-level 3D-printer demand remained strong in Q2 2025 while higher-end industrial segments faced headwinds. This establishes segment momentum but does not provide a free, audited U.S. accessory-market size. ([CONTEXT Q2 2025 market update](https://www.contextworld.com/en/quarterly-3d-printer-shipments-show-entry-level-remains-hot-while-industrial-segment-still-caught-in-catch-22))

- **Growth trend:** Strong entry-level momentum; rapid vendor and technical change.
- **Passion:** Very high among makers and problem solvers.
- **Repeat purchase:** High through filament, nozzles, build surfaces, storage, and maintenance.
- **Basket structure:** Low-cost consumables through expensive machines; AOV unknown.
- **Shipping complexity:** Low-medium for accessories/filament; higher for printers and chemicals.
- **Return risk:** Medium-high due to compatibility and user troubleshooting.
- **Review density:** Expected high and technically rich.
- **Supplier availability:** High, with concentrated low-cost manufacturing and fast imitation.
- **Content opportunity:** Exceptional but expertise-intensive and rapidly perishable.
- **Automation suitability:** Medium-low because printer/material/firmware compatibility changes.
- **Private-label potential:** Medium in consumable care and organization; quality consistency is critical.
- **Brand-building potential:** High around tested compatibility and failure prevention.
- **Recurring complaints:** Moisture-damaged filament, clogged nozzles, failed adhesion, calibration, warping, and unclear compatibility. UltiMaker documents moisture-related rough surfaces, weakened parts, jams, and failed prints. ([UltiMaker filament storage guidance](https://ultimaker.com/learn/3d-printer-filament-storage-essential-tips-and-ideas/))
- **Recurring wishes:** Reliable compatibility charts, fewer failed prints, standardized maintenance kits, and evidence-based settings.
- **Estimated validation cost:** **$0–$30** for technical review mining and five user failure diaries; physical testing would require separate approval.
- **Reasons not to enter:** Support burden, fast obsolescence, vendor concentration, firmware dependencies, safety/air-quality concerns, and demanding customers.
- **Cheapest meaningful experiment:** Collect five seven-day failure diaries from owners across three printer ecosystems and test whether a cross-platform pain cluster exists without machine-specific support.

**If we enter this market:** win on **tested failure prevention and compatibility evidence**, not novelty mods.

### 6. Pickleball

**Observed context.** SFIA reports 24.3 million U.S. participants in 2025, up from 4.2 million in 2020, and says pickleball has been the fastest-growing U.S. sport for five consecutive years. Participation growth is strong evidence; it is not proof of attractive accessory margins. ([SFIA 2026 Pickleball report release](https://sfia.org/resources/sfia-releases-2026-pickleball-single-sport-report-team-sports-reports-to-follow/))

- **Growth trend:** Exceptional recent participation growth; durability of the growth rate is uncertain.
- **Passion:** High and community-driven.
- **Repeat purchase:** Medium through balls, grips, protective items, apparel, and replacement equipment.
- **Basket structure:** Low-cost replenishment plus expensive paddles; AOV unknown.
- **Shipping complexity:** Low for most accessories.
- **Return risk:** Medium; feel, performance expectations, sizing, and paddle claims.
- **Review density:** Expected high and growing.
- **Supplier availability:** High; saturation risk is also high.
- **Content opportunity:** High—skill, rules, equipment selection, local community, and injury prevention.
- **Automation suitability:** High for simple compliant accessories.
- **Private-label potential:** Medium-high, though paddles face testing and approval requirements.
- **Brand-building potential:** Medium-high; harder without authentic play expertise.
- **Recurring complaints:** Durability, inconsistent balls, grip comfort, noise, unclear skill matching, and exaggerated paddle performance. These are hypotheses to quantify.
- **Recurring wishes:** Honest level matching, durable consumables, quieter play, and simplified beginner kits.
- **Estimated validation cost:** **$0–$25** for club interviews and authorized review analysis.
- **Reasons not to enter:** Crowded trend-chasing market, low differentiation, endorsement-heavy marketing, possible growth-rate reversion, and equipment-standard compliance.
- **Cheapest meaningful experiment:** Interview two club organizers and eight players across skill levels; test whether the same replenishment pain persists across indoor/outdoor settings.

**If we enter this market:** win on **level-appropriate, standards-aware reliability**, not hype or celebrity branding.

### 7. Freshwater Aquariums

**Observed context.** APPA estimates 10 million U.S. households own freshwater fish and describes increased engagement and more sophisticated habitats. It also highlights the importance of the in-store relationship, which may limit a pure online model. ([APPA Fish & Reptile Ownership 2025](https://americanpetproducts.org/news/from-bigger-tanks-to-stronger-bonds-fish-reptile-ownership-evolves-in-2025?hs_amp=true))

- **Growth trend:** Established hobby with engagement signals; public accessory growth is unknown.
- **Passion:** Very high among committed aquarists.
- **Repeat purchase:** Very high—food, test supplies, filter media, treatment, and replacement components.
- **Basket structure:** Strong replenishment plus costly system upgrades; AOV unknown.
- **Shipping complexity:** Low-medium for dry accessories; avoid livestock, liquids, glass, and bulky tanks initially.
- **Return risk:** Medium; compatibility and performance failures can harm animals.
- **Review density:** Expected very high and technically rich.
- **Supplier availability:** High, but product quality has animal-welfare consequences.
- **Content opportunity:** Exceptional—cycling, testing, diagnosis, compatibility, species needs, and maintenance.
- **Automation suitability:** Medium-low due to safety, compatibility, and support.
- **Private-label potential:** Medium for nonchemical accessories after testing.
- **Brand-building potential:** High around conservative, welfare-first guidance.
- **Recurring complaints:** Ammonia/nitrite spikes, cycling confusion, inaccurate tests, filter mismatch, algae, leaks, and contradictory advice. UF/IFAS identifies water quality, nutrition, sanitation, ammonia, nitrite, and pH as core health-management issues. ([UF/IFAS fish health management](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/FA004))
- **Recurring wishes:** Clear maintenance routines, trustworthy measurements, compatible replacement schedules, and early warning.
- **Estimated validation cost:** **$0–$25** for problem interviews and review classification; no live-animal experiment.
- **Reasons not to enter:** Animal welfare, chemical claims, emergency support expectations, product incompatibility, fragile/heavy goods, and customer harm from incorrect guidance.
- **Cheapest meaningful experiment:** Analyze beginner-failure narratives and test whether “measurement confidence” is a stable cross-system problem before evaluating any physical good.

**If we enter this market:** win on **welfare-first measurement and prevention**, not miracle fixes.

### 8. Backyard Chickens

Public participation estimates vary by methodology, so no household count is used as a decision fact here. - **Repeat purchase:** Very high—feed, bedding, sanitation, supplements, pest control, and replacement hardware.
- **Basket structure:** Recurring consumables plus large coop/hardware purchases; AOV unknown.
- **Shipping complexity:** Medium-high; feed/bedding are heavy, hardware bulky, treatments regulated.
- **Return risk:** Medium; fit, weather performance, predator resistance, and local suitability.
- **Review density:** Expected medium-high and problem-rich.
- **Supplier availability:** Medium-high across farm, pet, and direct channels.
- **Content opportunity:** Exceptional—daily care, seasonality, predators, disease, sanitation, local rules, and eggs.
- **Automation suitability:** Low-medium because climate, flock, regulation, and animal-health context vary.
- **Private-label potential:** Medium for non-medical compact accessories.
- **Brand-building potential:** High around practical prevention, but trust burden is substantial.
- **Recurring complaints:** Disease, odor, waste, pests, predators, wet feed, sanitation, and daily water/feed reliability. UMN Extension identifies disease, noise, odor, pests, and waste management as common concerns. ([UMN backyard poultry concerns](https://extension.umn.edu/small-scale-poultry/common-concerns-backyard-or-urban-poultry-keeping))

**If we enter this market:** win on **preventive daily systems and biosecurity clarity**, not homestead aesthetics.

### 9. Home Organization & Storage

**Observed context.** A July 2025 YouGov U.S. poll of 1,155 adults found 46% cited insufficient storage, 41% too many belongings, 37% difficulty letting go, 37% lack of motivation, 25% lack of time, and 23% not knowing where to start. Commonly used solutions included bins/containers (71%), shelving (54%), hooks (49%), and drawer dividers (31%). This directly establishes pain and behavior, not category revenue. ([YouGov Home Organization poll](https://ygo-assets-websites-editorial-emea.yougov.net/documents/Home_Organization_poll_results.pdf))

- **Growth trend:** Durable household problem; no reliable narrow-market growth estimate used.
- **Passion:** Medium; pain is broad even when enthusiasm is low.
- **Repeat purchase:** Medium-high across rooms, moves, family changes, and new constraints.
- **Basket structure:** Low-cost modular pieces with strong bundle potential; AOV unknown.
- **Shipping complexity:** Low for compact collapsible products; high for bulky rigid storage.
- **Return risk:** Medium due to dimensions, material quality, capacity, and visual mismatch.
- **Review density:** Expected very high.
- **Supplier availability:** Very high; commoditization is the central threat.
- **Content opportunity:** High—measurement, decision reduction, routines, before/after, and life-stage transitions.
- **Automation suitability:** Very high if fit guidance and dimensional data are excellent.
- **Private-label potential:** High, but only after proving a noncosmetic improvement.
- **Brand-building potential:** High around “buy fewer, fit better.”
- **Recurring complaints:** Insufficient space, too many possessions, lack of motivation/time, poor fit, weak materials, and systems that create more categories to maintain. The first four are directly supported by the YouGov poll; product-specific complaints need review validation.
- **Recurring wishes:** Exact fit confidence, visible contents, modularity, fewer decisions, durable materials, and routines the household will maintain.
- **Estimated validation cost:** **$0–$20** for space/problem interviews and a no-inventory concept test.
- **Reasons not to enter:** Extreme commodity competition, bulky shipping, easy copying, aesthetic subjectivity, and products that treat accumulation rather than the underlying behavior.
- **Cheapest meaningful experiment:** Ask 20 households to submit one frustrating space with measurements and current workaround; test whether one problem recurs across at least three room types without requiring custom installation.

**If we enter this market:** win on **fit certainty, restraint, and maintainable systems**, not selling more bins.

### 10. Kitchen Organization & Food Storage

**Observed context.** IHA’s 2025 Market Watch reported 58% of surveyed consumers planned to cook at home more, driven primarily by cost (61%) and health (59%); cleanup (51%) and meal prep (49%) were the leading reported cooking challenges. This supports a durable workflow problem, though the public release does not establish food-storage market revenue. ([International Housewares Association 2025 Market Watch](https://housewares.org/press-releases/2025-soi-market-watch/))

- **Growth trend:** Home-cooking intent is favorable; narrow organization growth is unknown.
- **Passion:** Medium-high; daily utility matters more than enthusiast identity.
- **Repeat purchase:** Medium-high through additional formats, wear, moves, and changing household needs.
- **Basket structure:** Low- to medium-ticket sets and replenishment; AOV unknown.
- **Shipping complexity:** Low-medium; avoid glass and oversized rigid systems initially.
- **Return risk:** Medium due to fit, leaks, odors, staining, lid mismatch, and cleaning.
- **Review density:** Expected very high.
- **Supplier availability:** Very high; differentiation and food-contact documentation are essential.
- **Content opportunity:** High—prep, cleanup, leftovers, pantry visibility, portioning, and waste reduction.
- **Automation suitability:** High with clear materials, dimensions, care, and food-contact compliance.
- **Private-label potential:** High after material and durability verification.
- **Brand-building potential:** High around less cleanup and fewer incompatible pieces.
- **Recurring complaints:** Cleanup burden and meal-prep friction are observed; leak resistance, lid mismatch, staining, odor retention, poor stacking, and cabinet fit are hypotheses to quantify.
- **Recurring wishes:** Interoperable lids, easy cleaning, space efficiency, visible contents, dependable seals, and fewer pieces.
- **Estimated validation cost:** **$0–$20** for workflow interviews and authorized review analysis.
- **Reasons not to enter:** Heavy saturation, food-contact compliance, entrenched brands, set-related returns, and substantial overlap with Home Organization.
- **Cheapest meaningful experiment:** Observe or interview ten households immediately after meal prep/cleanup and map friction by frequency, time cost, and current workaround—without discussing products.

**If we enter this market:** win on **interoperability and cleanup reduction**, not larger container sets.

## Why the other seven do not advance now

- **Kitchen Organization** is strategically attractive but is absorbed into Home Organization for the next sprint to avoid portfolio duplication.
- **Houseplants** is the first reserve. It has strong automation and content potential, but flat broad-industry growth and a crowded accessory field weaken immediate profit conviction.
- **Freshwater Aquariums** and **Backyard Chickens** offer excellent learning and repeat demand, but living-animal consequences raise the trust, support, and risk threshold for a first business.
- **Consumer 3D Printing** is the learning leader, but compatibility churn and troubleshooting burden make it a poor lean first-revenue candidate.
- **Pickleball** has extraordinary participation growth, but the current thesis is driven more by market momentum than by a verified unmet problem.
- **Van / RV Organization** has a credible installed base, but fragmented fit and installation increase validation and support costs.

Do not build a page. Do not choose a product. Collect comparable evidence for the three finalists.

For each finalist:

1. Acquire an authorized or user-provided corpus of **at least 100 relevant reviews** across at least three problem contexts. Preserve source, date, rating, and original text.
2. Conduct **five structured customer interviews** focused on the last problem, workaround, money/time lost, failed alternatives, and decision criteria.
3. Build a **supplier reality sample** of at least five plausible suppliers for the problem contexts—not outreach or purchasing, only public/authorized catalog evidence.
4. Produce a problem-frequency table, contradiction log, evidence-quality assessment, and reasons to reject.
5. Spend **$0 on advertising and inventory**. The recommended evidence-collection ceiling is **$25 per finalist**, but any spend still requires human approval.

### Elimination gate

A finalist survives only if it has:

- one recurring problem supported by both review evidence and interviews;
- a plausible nonregulated, compact, low-return solution space;
- at least two credible supplier paths;
- a reason customers would trust ACE over a commodity listing;
- a cheapest meaningful validation test within the existing $75 sprint ceiling;
- an explicit reason the opportunity may still fail.

If none survives, the correct decision is to advance none and revisit the reserve market. That is not sprint failure; it is capital protection.

## Source limitations

- Public trade-association summaries often sell more detailed reports; this sprint uses only claims visible in public material.
- Market-size figures refer to broad parent markets and should not be used in unit-economics calculations.
- No marketplace sales ranks, scraped reviews, paid keyword volumes, supplier quotations, return-rate datasets, or advertising benchmarks were used.
- Complaint lists are marked as observed when directly supported and otherwise remain hypotheses.
- Validation-cost ranges estimate our controllable research expense, not the cost to launch a business.
